    The problem

    Old and new satellite records don't match.

    NASA has tracked fires with the MODIS sensor since 2000 and with VIIRS since 2012. VIIRS detects smaller fires, so it reports far more burning for the same area. MODIS is retiring. Without a correction, records before 2012 cannot be compared with today.

    Uses

    Who needs a consistent fire record.

    The prototype covers Greater Houston. The method works anywhere NASA FIRMS has data.

    Emergency management

    Tell a normal burn week from the start of a bad one before committing crews.

    Forestry and land management

    Plan prescribed burns around the area's real fire season and keep a burn history.

    Agriculture

    Track crop-residue burning seasons and how they change from year to year.

    Electric utilities

    Find service areas where fire activity near power lines is above its usual level.

    Insurance and risk

    Assess wildfire exposure on 25 years of comparable history instead of 15.

    Air quality and public health

    Anticipate smoke seasons and relate burning to air-quality alerts.

    Method

    How it works.

    1. One gridEvery detection is placed on a 1 km grid. A square counts once per satellite per day.
    2. Gaps stay gapsA month counts only if the satellite was operating and every day was retrieved.
    3. Learn the conversionA model learns MODIS to VIIRS from 2012 to 2026, when both sensors flew.
    4. Rebuild 2000 to 2011Earlier months show the VIIRS-equivalent estimate with a likely range.
